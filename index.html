About

I work at the intersection of application security and the people who have to act on it — engineers fixing the vulnerability, and the stakeholders deciding what risk they're willing to carry.

At IBM, I own the DevSecOps security programme for a global manufacturing client: 8 applications across AWS, Azure, and on-premises infrastructure, instrumented with SAST, DAST, SCA, WAF, and secret detection across 6+ CI/CD pipelines. I lead delivery across 20+ security workstreams for that engagement and coordinate stakeholders across the Philippines and Indonesia — which means most of my week is spent translating the same finding three different ways: as a fix for the engineer who owns the code, as a risk statement for the client who owns the budget, and as a status update for the people in between.

The technical grounding behind that is direct, not theoretical. I've eliminated three recurring vulnerability classes — CORS misconfiguration, CSP gaps, and OAuth/JWT/SAML implementation errors — across 50+ findings, which means I've spent enough time inside these flows to know where they actually break, not just where a scanner flags them. That depth is also why I built and run a Security Champion programme that has trained 50+ engineers: you don't change how people write code with a compliance mandate, you change it by showing them the exploit.

Before IBM, I taught network security at the University of Maryland (M.Eng, Cybersecurity), designing and running 12 labs — cryptography, web security, OAuth/SAML — for 140+ students, rated 4.7/5.0. Teaching adversarial, technical material to people who don't yet have the vocabulary for it is the same discipline that consulting and presales work demands: know the material cold, then pitch it at the altitude the room actually needs.

I was awarded a Black Hat USA 2023 Student Scholarship, and hold Security+, ISC2 CC, and Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900), with an AWS certification in progress.

That work is less about finding vulnerabilities than about keeping a multi-geography programme moving: prioritising what gets fixed first, explaining why a finding matters to a team that didn't write the code, and keeping status legible to people several layers removed from the pipeline.
